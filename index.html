ABOUT THESE EXAMPLES

Listening & comparison notes

One pair, one listening setup

GT and Predicted use the same EARS speech context, FOA binaural decoder, HRTF and head pose. The supplied shared-gain audio is preserved without additional normalization. Dry speech is mono. Volume changes apply to every player.

Illustrations with traceable selection

Samples span rooms, directions and GT decay lengths. The lower-SNR SurrRoom example illustrates a challenging input condition. Selection does not use prediction error, and these six examples are not a statistical evaluation.

Signal preparation and interpretation

The audio files are the original shared_gain/gt_binaural.wav, shared_gain/a1_seed42_binaural.wav, and dry_ears.wav exports. All are 16 kHz, 4 seconds long. GT and prediction share the same gain and, in noisy cases, the same GT-calibrated added noise.

The dry clip is an aligned excerpt of the longer EARS context used for the original estimate. These examples demonstrate replay using that speech context; they do not establish reuse with unseen speech. A cropped wet signal can also contain reverberation from speech preceding the dry excerpt.

HARP uses native source geometry. SurrRoom examples use a common listening-only yaw rotation of paired FOA signals; their displayed directions are not distinct measured source orientations. Positive azimuth points left.

Source RIR channel order is ACN/SN3D [W, Y, Z, X]; the selector presents W, X, Y, Z. Plots preserve exported sample amplitudes. All spectrograms use a 512-sample Hann window, 128-sample hop, 512-point FFT, zero boundary padding and a fixed magnitude scale of −100 to 0 dB relative to 1 in the exported W reference units. The waveform amplitude axis is shared by all four channels and both time windows within each case.

The decay badge measures time from the aligned direct arrival to −20 dB of backward-integrated GT W energy over the available response. It is a descriptive selection measure, not a fitted RT60 value. CUBE is omitted from this edition because its shorter GT support needs a separately matched, truncated-response comparison.
